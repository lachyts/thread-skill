## Context

Once a budget is spent, a landing and a rollout task both wait for Lachy, and in both his answer is mechanical:

- **A landing** (close's own-branch loop) has three code rounds. A `ci failed`, or a red Verify run after a clean round, once they are spent is "a decision for Lachy" (close § The stop rule), even a flake, and `--fixed-at-budget` refuses an untrailered fix, so whatever he says he merges by hand. A red run with no cause in the branch, or one also red on `origin/<default>`, is a decision for him at any count, and so is an unconfirmed round.
- **A rollout task** has `auto_retries` (2) automatic retries, and round caps: `max_review_rounds`, `max_plan_rounds` and `max_iterations`. After them, a set-aside task waits for `/thread:repair`'s `record-answer --raise` on his word (ADR 0035's H9 `raise <field>, hand back or defer`), which approves one more round, or for a bare hand-back (H10) that approves one more retry. A quota block past its five free retries waits for him the same way (ADR 0033 d5).

His Priorities card says the project runs without him, and since 2026-11-06 that approving another round or run is never his question.

## Decision

1. **Overtime.** Once a landing's or a rollout task's normal budget is spent, it takes up to `overtime_runs` more runs by itself (built-in 3, Lachy's "3 extra runs" of 2026-11-06; decision 8), never asked. The normal budget is a landing's three code rounds, and a rollout task's `auto_retries` with the round cap its stop spent (`max_review_rounds`, `max_plan_rounds` or `max_iterations`). Two landing stops have no normal budget of their own, so they draw on the same allowance from their first run, at any round: a re-run of a failure with no cause in the change, which spends no round, and a fix attempt at a blocking finding, which no next round can follow while it is open (decision 3). An Overtime run is one of:
   - **a re-run**: a failed check or Verify run run again on the same tree, with no commit of a fix (decision 3 says which failures);
   - **a fix**: a commit that fixes a `ci failed` or red Verify run, with the one review round that reads it;
   - **a fix attempt**: one more attempt, with no review round, at a confirmed blocking finding's fix that has not passed its reproduction, committed with its reproduction only once that passes and loop step 7 is green; on the budget's round or later nothing reviews it again (ADR 0065 Decision 3, and decision 9), and before it the next round reads it as it reads any fix;
   - **a review round**: one more code round of a landing's chain (decision 9), or one more master-review round of an engine task, through `max_review_rounds` raised by one;
   - **a plan round**: one more plan round, through `max_plan_rounds` raised by one;
   - **a verifier pass**: one more verifier iteration, through `max_iterations` raised by one;
   - **a retry**: one more automatic re-entry of a set-aside task past `auto_retries`, an infra or quota block's included (decision 3).

   **One counting rule.** An Overtime run is one re-entry from a stop to the next stop or merge, whatever it holds: a fix with its review round is one, a revise with its review round is one, a fix attempt is one, a re-run is one. In the engine one re-entry is one `auto-retry` verb call, raising by one the cap its stop spent; in a landing it is one record (decision 2).
2. **Where the count lives.** The count is durable: only `defer` resets a task's count, and a landing's never resets.
   - **A rollout task**: an `overtime_used` frontmatter marker on its task note, written by `reconcile-rollout.py auto-retry` in the save that re-enters it. An Overtime re-entry is always that verb's, never `hand-back`'s, and `hand-back` (ADR 0033 d6), a recorded answer and a supersede's `carry` leave the marker as it stands: a human answer gives back the normal budget, never the Overtime. Only `defer` clears it, whichever defer it is: a take-out's (decision 5), which starts the task's next life under decision 6's count, or a repair § 5 defer on Lachy's word, which is no take-out and counts none.
   - **A landing**: each Overtime run commits one record under `docs/reviews/` on the branch (`mode: overtime`, `overtime: <n>`, its kind and its key), then a `docs/reviews/`-only commit deleting it, and pushes both by one plain call before the run starts; an Overtime review round's own doc carries `overtime: <n>` too. The record is deleted in the push that carries it, never left for the deletion commit close makes before `--queue` (close § The review docs): that later commit would move HEAD after the run close waited on, so `--queue`, which pushes before it reads, would read a newer HEAD whose run is still pending. The push moves HEAD, so it is also the re-run of every check it triggers (§ Decided for him, A re-run, in order). A resumed landing (a hold, a lost session, a later plain call's `stuck:`) reads the highest `overtime:` among the docs added since its PR opened, deleted ones included (`--diff-filter=A` reads the commit that added each), by close's own Counting command with `overtime` for `round`, so no session gets a fresh three. Nothing resets it: a landing's take-out is a hold (decision 5), and its PR, still open, keeps its count.
3. **Which stops run Overtime.** Every stop that only approves another run is an Overtime run once the normal budget is spent (a landing's re-run and fix attempt at any round, decision 1), and is never asked:
   - a `ci failed`, or a red Verify run, after a clean round once the landing's budget is spent: a fix;
   - a failure with no cause in the change, a flake, a runner problem or one also red on `origin/<default>`: a re-run, before the budget or past it, and never a stop for Lachy (his ruling of 2026-11-12, decided for him: any machinery snag is the system's). Each re-run spends one Overtime run, from the first: a re-run costs no round, so the allowance is all that bounds it, and a failure that keeps coming back on the same tree (a red Verify run also red on master, which close's loop step 7 never lets past to a round) is taken out once the Overtime is spent, never re-run without end. Before the budget the re-run takes the place of today's next round on an unchanged tree after a no-cause `ci failed`. One red on master is also the master-runs reading's red-master fix (ADR 0084);
   - a confirmed blocking finding whose fix cannot be made to pass its reproduction, at any round: a fix attempt, each attempt spending one Overtime run, so up to `overtime_runs` attempts, then the take-out once they are spent with the reproduction still failing;
   - an unconfirmed round (a checker lost twice): a review round;
   - an engine task past its `auto_retries` with its round cap spent or not (`lead-integrate.py`'s verdict cause 24, ADR 0035's H9 or H10 for it): a retry, with the raise of the cap its stop spent;
   - an infra block past `auto_retries`: a retry, after the 240-minute cool-down (ADR 0033 d5), so an infra loop is bounded by the Overtime;
   - a quota block past its five free retries (cause 22): a retry, after the 480-minute cool-down.

   A landing's CI infrastructure conclusions (`cancelled`, `timed_out`, `startup_failure` and the like) still never count towards a key or raise effort (close § The stop rule's CI key), but each re-run of one spends an Overtime run, like any re-run above, so a `timed_out` the change causes (a test that hangs) is bounded too.
4. **His decisions still stop.** A decision only Lachy can make still reaches him as it does today, at any point in the budget or the Overtime, and spends nothing: a product or design call, an ADR change, or a reproduction close believes shows intended behaviour; a blocking fix outside the task; a gate, a credential or an irreversible sign-off (ADR 0008); a `## Needs you` question; an undecided RACE or UNVERIFIED; a refused or repeated descope (H5); an Integration set-aside with no `pr:`, a gone branch or a `pr:` the engine refuses (H6 to H8); merge-task's exit-1 texts outside ADR 0033 d8's allowlist (cause 15); the same Integration stop twice running (cause 23), whose fix is outside the task (a runner, a GitHub setting); a merge setting left for him (H13); an invalid round-budget stamp (H12). Two landing stops are his for the same reason: `max unavailable` (a spawn-depth setting only he changes) and a dirty tree (it may hold his own work). The Overtime replaces only approvals of another run.

   Every other verdict cause of `lead-integrate.py` keeps its Human fix as it stands: only causes 18, 22, 24 and 25 change (§ Consequences). Causes 3 (H11, a code-writing review note with no `pr:`) and 4 (H2, no rollout note) leave nothing the engine can re-enter, as H6 to H8 do; cause 14, a `--gated` decline, is his own gate; cause 22a keeps ADR 0080's settings route, its H13 above. Causes 17 (an unresolvable budget), 19 (no feedback fingerprint), 20 (malformed counters) and 21 (a malformed `auto_retry_at`) are fail-closed states and stay with him: the engine cannot read the budget, the count, the cool-down's stamp or the fingerprint its Overtime and its take-out rest on, so it neither re-enters nor takes out, and his answer mends the note before any run, never only approving one.
5. **Then it is taken out.** With the Overtime spent and the work still not mergeable, or at once on the same feedback again (§ Decided for him), the work is taken out, and nobody is asked:
   - **A rollout task** leaves the rollout by repair § 5's clean defer, its retire block included: its worktree is removed, its integration anchor deleted and its PR closed with `--delete-branch`. GitHub keeps a closed PR's diff and commits (`refs/pull/<n>/head`), so the PR link stays the evidence, and the next rollout starts on a fresh branch, colliding with no old branch or worktree. The rollout runs on to its completion without it.
   - **Its dependants.** Its dependent closure (repair § 5 step 1: transitive, through `merged_into:` tombstones) leaves with it, each by the same clean defer, leaves first, so the take-out is a chain take-out and no ask. A dependant running or integrating under a live lead, or held by a serial Leg, is never written: it finishes first (his ruling of 2026-11-12, decided for him: finished work is not thrown away), and the take-out waits for it. A dependant whose merge lands the task's own commits (a stacked PR) merges the task through it, and nothing is taken out; any other outcome takes the task and the closure that remains out.
   - **A landing** is held: its PR stays open with auto-merge off, and its evidence goes into the landing's one follow-up task (the filed-findings task of ADR 0058 Decision 2, written then if the landing has none), so a landing still writes at most one task nobody asked for.
   - **The evidence stays out of the brief.** A take-out writes nothing into the task note's body, which descope's `_brief_items` and the plan judge read (ADR 0033's rejected option). It writes a stamped line in the rollout's `## Notes` (`- <stamp> take-out: [[<slug>]] after <n> Overtime runs (<stage>; <the last diagnosis's first line>; <pr url>)`), a `take-out` Run record event, and two frontmatter markers that `defer` never clears: `taken_out` (how many times, across rollouts) and `taken_out_of` (the rollouts), from which a later reader finds each take-out's evidence.
6. **A task taken out more than once.** The first take-out puts the task back in the open backlog, and the next orient places it again. The second marks it for a rewrite: the next Shaping session (orient, or orient-all's pass) splits, narrows or restates it from both take-outs' evidence, and it gets one more rollout; schedule's Pickup sweep never adds a marked task before its rewrite. The third puts one row on his needs-you list with every take-out's diagnosis and a recommendation. (Lachy, 2026-11-12: "Rewritten first, then me." Built by [[thread-skill-p39-21-a-task-taken-out-twice-is-rewritten-then-reaches-him]].)
7. **Listed, never alerted.** A taken-out task shows in the rollout's report (or close's landing row), its Run record and the weekly Reflection. It is no needs-you item until decision 6's third take-out, and no rollout pushes anything anyway (ADR 0075). (Lachy, 2026-11-06: "Listed, no alert".)
8. **The allowance is a Tuning key.** `overtime_runs` (an integer ≥ 0, built-in 3) resolves as `auto_retries` does, task note → rollout note → `~/.config/thread/rollouts.toml` (`[defaults]` or a repo table) → built-in, through `rollout-settings.py`, and a landing reads its repo's value the same way. It joins `TUNING_KEYS`, so the weekly reading may land it as an agreed knob, and revert it, unasked, inside the Guardrails like any knob (ADR 0037 d5). (Lachy's ruling of 2026-11-12, decided for him by his Note of 2026-11-06: "if all I'm doing is approving another review round or another kind of run, I don't want to be asked".) `auto_retries` stays no Tuning key, ADR 0033's rejected option standing for it.
9. **A landing's Overtime review rounds are rounds four and on.** The landing's chain goes on past `round: 3` only for an Overtime round: close names it as one when it invokes `/fresh-review`, and its doc says `round: <n>` (4 or more) and `overtime: <k>`. fresh-review's Step 2 check 3, "no fourth", is amended to dispatch such a round and no other, in `~/.agents` by [[fresh-review-skill-followup-a-landing-overtime-round-past-the-third]]: p39-13 waits for it. An Overtime round is read as the budget round is: a blocking finding on it is fixed until its reproduction passes, and land.sh's `--fixed-at-budget` already reads every doc whose `round:` is 3 or more.

## Decided for him (overturnable)

- One counting rule → decision 1's: one re-entry from a stop to the next stop or merge is one run, whatever it holds (technical: an engine revise and its review round are one re-entry, so "three" means three in the engine and the landing alike, and p39-14's "raised `max_review_rounds` up to three times" reads the same).
- The same feedback again → taken out at once, without spending the rest of the Overtime, before the budget is spent or after: an engine agent block whose fingerprint equals `auto_retry_sha` (cause 25), or a landing fix whose key has failed twice at `max` (technical: another identical run cannot change the outcome, and it is no decision only he can make). A re-run is exempt, since a re-run is how a flake clears; the Overtime it spends from the first bounds it (decision 3).
- A re-run, in order → one sequence, each step resting on the one before it (technical: land.sh reads only the reviewed commit R and the HEAD `--queue` pushes, and GitHub's auto-merge only that HEAD, so a run anywhere else is read by nothing; gh re-runs only Actions runs):
  1. **One push.** Decision 2's record and its deletion go up in one plain call, which also switches auto-merge off and withdraws the queue marker. The new HEAD H gets a fresh run of every check the push triggers, an Actions run, a commit status and a third-party check app alike. H is a `docs/reviews/`-only push like the old HEAD's (the deletion commit the last `--queue` pushed), so every check that ran on the old HEAD runs on H: for a failure on the old HEAD, H's run is the re-run, and nothing reads the old HEAD again. No commit follows H before `--queue`, so the HEAD close waits on is the HEAD `--queue` reads.
  2. **R's failed run, re-run too.** When R's newest run of the failed check is a failed Actions run, close re-runs it (`gh run rerun --failed <run id>`, the run R's check runs name), whichever commit land.sh's line named, so R's newest run of the check is the re-run, never the failure: land.sh lets only H's finished pass supersede a failure on R, so otherwise R's failure would stand against H's run while it is pending. For an Actions check the push does not trigger on H (a path filter that skips a `docs/reviews/`-only push), it is the only re-run.
  3. **Wait on the runs themselves.** Close never polls: it waits on H's run of the failed check (`gh run watch <id> --exit-status` for an Actions run, `gh pr checks <url> --watch` for a status or an app check) and on R's re-run when step 2 started one (`gh run watch <id> --exit-status`), each wait bounded by a liveness limit (technical: one snapshot read at once would see the re-run pending and switch auto-merge on).
  4. **Then `--queue`.** Its push sends nothing new, and land.sh, unchanged (Reading a re-run, below), reads the runs close waited on: H's finished pass supersedes a failure on R, H's finished failure decides, and a check H does not run is read from R's re-run alone. A `ci failed` here is a new failure, its own stop, and a re-run of it spends another Overtime run.
  5. **A wait cut by its bound.** `--queue` reads a pending run on H as pending, but a failure on R stands against it, and a pending run on R is not read at all. So a cut wait calls `--queue` only when the run still going is H's and R's newest run of the check is no failure (step 2 replaced it, or the failure was the old HEAD's alone): the landing ends `queued`, auto-merge on and GitHub merging on H's pass, the record already pushed for the next session to count (a re-run already finished red reads `ci failed`, as in step 4). Otherwise the wait ends without `--queue`: for a non-Actions check whose newest run on R failed, which no re-run can replace, R's failure would read `ci failed`, a new failure where CI is only slow; for an Actions check H does not run, its re-run on R would go unread, and the landing could merge before it finishes. Auto-merge stays off, as step 1's plain call left it, the landing's row names the check still running, and the next close on `<B>` queues it once that run has finished, its count read from the record. The landing carrier (`land-carrier.py`, ADR 0078 d2) never queues it: it leaves an unqueued landing to its session (its step 6.0) and lists it as stuck after a day (its step 7).
  6. **No run to make.** A non-Actions check whose newest run on R failed and that does not run on H cannot be re-run anywhere land.sh reads, so no run can change the result: the landing is taken out at once (a hold, decision 5), as for the same feedback again.

  A red Verify run's re-run needs none of the CI steps: after step 1's push, close runs loop step 7 again in `top` on the same tree.
- Reading a re-run → land.sh as it stands, unchanged: it keeps each check's newest run on a commit, sorted by id, so a re-run on R replaces R's failure; a finished pass on HEAD supersedes R's failure; and HEAD's own finished failure stays the deciding result, since HEAD is the commit GitHub's auto-merge reads (technical: `LAND_CHECKS_PY` already reads a re-run, and a rule of "the newest completed run on R or HEAD", this bullet's first text, would have let a green re-run on R beat a finished failure on HEAD).
- `auto_retries = 0`, or `overtime_runs = 0` → no Overtime: the stop that spends the normal budget is a take-out at once, never a re-entry and never his, and so is a landing's first failure with no cause in it and its first blocking fix that cannot pass (technical: ADR 0033 d3's zero turns every automatic retry off, and asking him to approve the run is what this ADR ends).
- A take-out's retire block → repair § 5's whole block runs (technical: a kept branch or worktree collides with the next rollout's dispatch of the same slug, and a closed PR stays readable).
- A take-out's evidence → the rollout's `## Notes`, the Run record and two markers, never the task body (technical: ADR 0033 keeps records out of the brief).
- A held landing's evidence → folded into its one follow-up task, never a second task (technical: close's guardrail allows exactly one task nobody asked for).
- The term → **Overtime**, since "overrun" already names the Night record's "fallback overrun" in the weekly Reflection that will list take-outs; "raise" stays a live word: the Human fix's `raise` and ADR 0033 d7's automatic raise both stand, and an engine Overtime run's extra round is a raise (technical).
- Dev-time `/fresh-review` rounds → never count towards a landing's three or its Overtime (priorities: ADR 0065 already rules the landing's chain is its own; the Counting command is brought into line).
- A CI fix past the budget → its commit is reviewed by its one Overtime round before the merge is queued (priorities: production-ready, so no unreviewed fix lands).

## Considered options

- **Stop for Lachy on a failure with no cause in the change** (close's rule today). Rejected by his ruling of 2026-11-12: a machinery snag is the system's, and re-running a red-on-master check costs at most the Overtime before the take-out.
- **Infrastructure blocks never counting against the Overtime.** Rejected: ADR 0033 d5 has them spend `auto_retries`, and exempting them past it would retry a dying call every 240 minutes forever.
- **Deferring dependants as they stand, running or not.** Rejected: repair § 5 never writes a task a live lead is running, and a dependant about to merge is finished work.
- **Re-placing a taken-out task with no bound, or alerting him at the first take-out.** Rejected by his answer to #6: rewritten first, then him.
- **A fixed three.** Rejected by his ruling on #15: tuning the allowance is another approval he does not want.
- **Keeping the PR and branch open at a take-out.** Rejected: the next rollout's dispatch of the slug collides with them, and GitHub keeps a closed PR readable.
- **Writing the evidence into the task note.** Rejected, as ADR 0033 rejected it: the body is the brief.
- **A take-out task of its own for a held landing.** Rejected: close's guardrail allows one unsolicited task per landing, and its follow-up task can carry both.
- **Counting a fix and its review round as two runs.** Rejected: the engine and the landing would build different threes.
- **Keeping the name Overrun.** Rejected: two unrelated mechanisms under one word in one weekly Reflection.
